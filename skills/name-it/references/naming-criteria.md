# Naming Criteria

## Hard requirements
- **Single word.** No hyphenated compounds, no "-App"/"-Hub"/"-ly"/"-ify" suffixes bolted on just to make a domain available.
- **Easy to say and spell.** If someone would need it spelled out letter-by-letter over a call, it fails this check. Avoid unusual letter combinations, silent letters, or ambiguous pronunciation.
- **Works as a domain/subdomain.** Should read cleanly as `name.tld` or `project.hiroshiyoka.xyz` without looking cramped or requiring a hyphen.

## Strong preferences
- **Evocative, not generic.** The name should hint at the project's core function or feeling. A name that could equally apply to any app in the category (e.g. "Flow" for literally anything) is weaker than one tied to something specific about this project.
- **Short.** Prefer 4-7 letters where possible — shorter names are easier to remember and type, though this isn't an absolute cutoff if a longer word is otherwise excellent.
- **Real word or near-real word** over invented/portmanteau names, unless a genuine invented word is unusually strong. Real words carry existing connotations for free.

## Process
1. List 3-5 core concepts/metaphors from the project description first (e.g. for an LDR app: distance, connection, waiting, weaving, threads, tides).
2. For each concept, brainstorm 2-3 candidate words.
3. Filter against the hard requirements above.
4. Narrow to the 5-8 strongest survivors and present as the shortlist.

## Examples from prior projects (for calibration, not to reuse)
- **Loom** — LDR app; "weaves" two people's lives together across distance.
- **Farwell** — breakup accountability app; play on "farewell," signals moving on.
- **Trackr** — job application tracker; plain, functional, no forced metaphor needed since it's a utility tool.
- **Nomi** — productivity dashboard; short, easy to say, no strained metaphor.
- **Owe** — expense-splitting app; literal and immediately understandable.
- **Drip** — tipping/saweran app; evokes a steady trickle of small payments.
- **Proven** — on-chain reputation app; literal, states exactly what it does.
